# Code of conduct

cvault is a feature-frozen learning project (see the [README](README.md)), so there is no
active community to manage. This short code still applies wherever the project is
discussed: its issues, pull requests, discussions and any other space that uses its
name.

## Expectations

Everyone is expected to:

- be respectful and patient, especially with people who are learning;
- assume good faith, and disagree about ideas without attacking people;
- keep feedback specific and constructive, and criticise the code, not the author;
- respect other people's privacy and never publish personal information or
  credentials that are not yours.

The following is not acceptable: harassment, discrimination or insults of any kind,
sexualised language or attention, threats, personal attacks, spam, and posting other
people's private information.

## Scope

This applies to contributors, maintainers and anyone who takes part in a space that
represents the project. Behaviour in other places is outside its scope unless it
targets people in those spaces.

## Enforcement

The maintainer may edit or remove comments, close or lock threads and block accounts
that do not respect these expectations, without notice and without a duty to explain.
Because nobody is monitoring the repository around the clock, enforcement can be slow.

To report a problem, use GitHub's *Report content* option on the comment, or contact
the maintainer through the [profile of the repository owner](https://github.com/t-aize).
Reports are treated with discretion.

## Forks

If you fork cvault and run your own community, you are free to adopt this text or a
stronger one, such as the
[Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/), which inspired it.
